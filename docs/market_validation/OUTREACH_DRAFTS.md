## Reddit value post

**Title:** Finance teams with messy month-end exports: what part of reconciliation still needs a human detective?

I am testing a local-first reconciliation workflow for Finance Managers who receive several Excel/CSV exports at month-end and still have to explain why a subledger, sales report, marketplace payout or subsidiary file does not tie to the GL.

The design rule is intentionally conservative: uncertain column mappings stop for review, currency conversion requires an approved dated policy, and the tool never creates a balancing plug. It should point to the exact source rows and a controlled reason such as timing, split/merged postings, tax, fees, refunds or FX. The output remains on the user's machine.

Before building more, I want to understand the work that current tools still leave behind. If you own a monthly reconciliation, I would be grateful for a 30-minute walkthrough of the last real exception you investigated:

- which exports and systems were involved;
- what changed or failed;
- how long the investigation took;
- what evidence the reviewer needed;
- and what cannot leave your machine.

No company data is required for the conversation. For a private pilot, anonymized headers, 20-50 representative rows and trusted totals are enough. I am looking for a small number of design partners willing to test the same workflow across two close cycles, not for a sales call.

## X thread

1/ A Sales report can differ from the GL by a small amount for many legitimate reasons: a split posting, a batch, tax, fees, a credit note, FX, or a period cut-off. "AI commentary" is not useful if the number was never reconciled.

2/ The workflow Finance needs is evidence-first: preserve every source row, stop on uncertain mappings, apply only an approved dated currency policy, and show which records explain the variance.

3/ A useful month-end tool should say "these records create the difference" or "this remains unresolved." It should never invent a plug so the pack appears balanced.

4/ I am building FinCompiler around that principle. It runs locally from Excel/CSV exports and keeps the calculation and lineage trail on the user's machine.
